# Wranngle UX Roast

Date: 2026-05-20 local / 2026-05-21 UTC  
App: `http://localhost:5173/` via `bun run dev`  
Evidence: `.artifacts/ux-roast-2026-05-20/`

I clicked through the home page, desktop nav menus, primary CTA, intake fork,
mock agent demo, offerings cards, product pages, about, pricing, pilot, legal
pages, 404, and mobile views at 390px. Screenshots and text dumps are saved in
the artifacts folder, with `contact-sheet.jpg` as the quick overview.

## Princess Verdict

The site looks deliberate and expensive, but it also looks like it has been
locked in a terminal bunker and fed only orange buttons. The brand is memorable;
the buying path is less so. A real skeptical visitor gets the vibe quickly, then
has to excavate what package exists, what it costs, whether Sarah is the product
or a popup roommate, and why every page is yelling "live console" at the same
volume.

Spiritually: confident, technical, slightly haunted by its own aesthetic.

## Highest Priority Problems

1. **Sarah is everywhere, including places she should shut up.**  
   On desktop, the ElevenAgents widget stays visible while modals and spec
   sheets are open. On mobile, it collides with the opened menu and sits on top
   of the CTA area. In `20-mobile-menu.png`, the orange menu CTA is partially
   hidden behind the Sarah widget. In `21-mobile-cta-dialog.png`, the widget
   competes with the modal the user just opened. This feels broken, not premium.
   Hide or minimize Sarah whenever the mobile nav, intake modal, or spec sheet
   is open. Better: render a small launcher until the user explicitly asks for
   Sarah.

2. **The main CTA adds a decision instead of removing one.**  
   Clicking "Build my call flow" / "Get call coverage" first asks "Talk it out
   with Sarah" vs "Fill out the form" (`04-primary-cta-dialog.png`). I already
   clicked the primary CTA. Making me choose another path is friction dressed as
   helpfulness. Default the CTA to the intake form. Put Sarah as a secondary
   action inside the form or after submission.

3. **Pricing and package names do not reconcile.**  
   Home sells Core Agent and Elite Agent around `$250/mo` and `$500/mo`, while
   `/pricing` sells Starter `$299/month`, Professional `$899/month`, and
   Enterprise custom (`14-pricing-top.png`). A visitor cannot tell whether these
   are the same offer, a newer offer, or a pricing jump hiding behind nicer
   names. This is the biggest trust leak. Pick one package system or add an
   explicit mapping.

4. **The mobile menu is visually compromised.**  
   The menu itself is clean, but the Sarah widget overlaps the bottom CTA, so
   the most important action looks like it has been shoved behind another
   product (`20-mobile-menu.png`). A mobile visitor gets "Home, About,
   Offerings, Talk to Sarah, also another Talk to Sarah, and some orange slab
   trapped under it." This needs a z-index/state policy, not another tweak.

5. **The intake modal is too much ceremony before the user has committed.**  
   The form view (`05-primary-cta-form-or-next.png`) starts with "Elite Agent
   selected", immediately cross-sells a landing page, then finally asks for
   business basics. That is sales-desk energy before I even type my name. Let
   the user complete the core lead form first. Cross-sell after capture or in a
   quieter sidebar.

6. **"LIVE" signals are risky when the data is fixture-backed.**  
   The live bookings module displays fresh-looking rows while the console log
   shows the upstream ticker failed with CORS and local `/api/ticker` 404. The
   UI silently falls back to deterministic fixture rows. Fine for resilience,
   dangerous for trust. If it is degraded, either do not call it live, or expose
   a subtle "sample activity" state.

## Visual Roast

- The home hero is strong at first glance: bold headline, confident brand, good
  dark texture (`01-home-top-desktop.png`). Then the second screen is another
  brand/logo media block, then a console, then ticker, then more console. The
  visitor is not discovering new proof so much as walking through the same room
  with different labels.
- The palette is distinctive but relentless. Burgundy, black, orange, repeat.
  It is memorable, but after five pages it starts to feel like every product is
  the same product in a different jacket.
- The Websites page is the clearest product page (`10-websites-top.png`). It
  says what it sells, gives a simple promise, and the hero mockup is readable.
  It should be the benchmark for the other pages.
- The `gtm_ops` page has the best real artifact because it shows an actual
  console (`11-gtm-ops-top.png`), but the product still feels insider-y. I can
  admire it before I understand whether I personally need it.
- The About page is polished, but it is more resume than conversion surface
  (`13-about-top.png`). The first viewport says "Cody Arnold" before it says why
  I should trust Wranngle with revenue-critical calls.
- The legal pages look surprisingly on-brand, but the sticky marketing header
  plus "Get call coverage" CTA on Privacy/Terms feels aggressive for a user who
  may just be checking terms.

## Functional Notes

- Desktop mega menus work when clicked directly, but the Offerings menu is huge
  and covers the H1 (`tmp-offerings-menu.png`). It looks like a black shelf fell
  out of the nav. Also, entire columns are clickable links; that is clever but
  easy to trigger accidentally when trying to inspect tiers.
- The About menu is cleaner (`tmp-about-menu.png`), though "Talk to Sarah" is
  visually heavier than "About Wranngle", so the nav pushes demo over company
  context.
- The AI agent spec sheet is useful and visually punchy
  (`tmp-spec-sheet-real.png`), but the close `x` nearly disappears on the white
  sheet and Sarah still floats nearby like an uninvited accessory.
- The mocked agent demo is more honest than the external Sarah widget because it
  says no mic/sign-up and shows the scripted call. Consider making this the
  primary demo surface and demoting the third-party widget.
- No horizontal overflow showed up in the desktop or mobile screenshots.
- The 404 is fine and on-brand (`18-404-top.png`).

## Console / Network Issues Captured

- React warning: `fetchPriority` is passed as an unrecognized prop on the logo
  image. Use `fetchpriority` or remove it depending on React support.
- Ticker fetch from `https://app.wranngle.com/api/ticker` failed due CORS in
  local dev, then `/api/ticker` returned 404.
- `THREE.Clock` deprecation warning appears from the 3D/orb stack.
- Google Fonts were blocked by the headless browser environment, so I would not
  treat that one as product evidence without verifying in a normal browser.

## What I Would Fix First

1. Hide Sarah during nav/menu/modal states and use a small launcher by default.
2. Make the primary CTA open the intake form directly.
3. Unify the package/pricing language across Home, `/pricing`, and product
   pages.
4. Label ticker fallback state honestly or fix the upstream CORS contract.
5. Make each product route visually distinct: AI voice can be call flow,
   Websites can stay build/lead-capture, `gtm_ops` can be actual console/proposal
   proof.
6. Move cross-sells out of the first intake viewport.
